What the word means

Ishtabhriti (ইষ্টভৃতি, also written Istavrity or Istabhriti) joins two words: Ishta, the beloved Ideal, and bhriti, sustaining or nourishing. It is the daily practice of offering something, with love, to the Ideal before attending to one's own needs.

Sri Sri Thakur placed it at the centre of daily devotion. It is meant less as a ritual than as a habit of putting the Ideal first, every day, so that love for the Ideal shapes the rest of one's life.

How it sits with the other practices

  • Jajan

    Cultivating oneself through spiritual practice, study and living by one's ideals.

  • Yajan

    Sharing what one has received with others, through conduct and conversation.

  • Swastyayani

    Vows for wellbeing that tie one's own health and character to service of others.

Together they are often spoken of as the daily practices of a Satsangi. See all six core ideas

Learning the practice

Ishtabhriti is usually taken up after initiation, and the way it is done is taught personally. If you wish to begin, speak to a ritwik or the people at your nearest Satsang Vihar, which you can find on the Global Map.
